Advanced Causal Inference Report

Step 7 — Regression, IPTW, Doubly Robust, Bootstrap, and HTE Analysis

Metformin users vs Other diabetes medication users | Full cohort N = 69 | Matched N = 16

1. Study Objective

Estimate the causal effect of diabetes pharmacotherapy (metformin vs other diabetes medications) on the composite outcome of diabetes-related complications or hospitalization using advanced observational causal inference methods appropriate for pharmaceutical real-world evidence.

2. Causal Question

What is the causal effect of diabetes treatment on clinical outcomes?

Treatment (Metformin vs Other) → Outcome (complication / hospitalization)
Adjusted for: Age, Gender (female), HbA1c (%), BMI (kg/m2), Hypertension, Cardiovascular disease, Obesity, Hyperlipidemia, Kidney disease

3. DAG and Causal Assumptions

The causal directed acyclic graph posits that age, gender, disease severity (partially unmeasured), and comorbidities confound the treatment–outcome relationship. Comorbidity burden proxies disease severity.

  • Exchangeability: Conditional exchangeability: treatment assignment is independent of potential outcomes given measured baseline covariates (no unmeasured confounding given X).
  • Positivity: Positivity (overlap): each patient has non-zero probability of receiving either metformin or other diabetes medications within strata of measured confounders.
  • Consistency: Consistency: observed outcome equals the potential outcome under the treatment actually received; no interference between patients.

4. Confounder Adjustment Strategy

All baseline covariates were measured at or before treatment initiation (index date). Adjustment strategies include multivariable regression, propensity score IPTW (ATE and ATT), doubly robust AIPW, DoWhy backdoor identification, and EconML double machine learning.

5. Statistical Methods

  • Multivariable logistic regression: Outcome ~ Treatment + Covariates
  • Stabilized IPTW for ATE; ATT weights for treated population
  • Doubly robust augmented inverse-probability weighting (AIPW)
  • Bootstrap resampling (500 iterations) for IPTW ATE uncertainty
  • DoWhy backdoor propensity score weighting
  • EconML LinearDML with gradient boosting nuisance models
  • Sensitivity: drop-one covariate, alternative PS models, matching calipers
  • Subgroup analyses: age group, gender, comorbidity burden

8. Limitations

  • Small sample (N=69) limits precision and subgroup analyses.
  • Disease severity is partially captured via baseline HbA1c, BMI, and comorbidity burden; insulin resistance and adherence remain unmeasured.
  • Residual confounding from unmeasured clinical and behavioral factors may remain despite lab adjustment.
  • Synthea synthetic data may not reflect real-world treatment pathways.
  • Positivity violations in extreme PS strata can destabilize weighting.
Results estimate the causal effect under assumptions of no unmeasured confounding, correct model specification, and adequate overlap of treatment groups.
